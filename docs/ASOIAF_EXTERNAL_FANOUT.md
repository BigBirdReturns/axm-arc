# ASOIAF external-source fanout

The qualified source atlas is the shared control plane for three implementation branches. Each branch inherits the same source identities, authority classes, rights modes, candidate routes, collection limits, receipt law, and promotion firewall. None may redefine the atlas locally.

## Collector estate

`feature/asoiaf-external-collector-v1` owns bounded acquisition and local custody. It materializes the source ledger, append-only observations, candidates, gaps, attempts, receipts, caches, and selective public credits. It implements the UnderCast operating loop and the Clifford Number observation boundary. Its scheduled jobs may collect or refresh source records, but they cannot write canon, review decisions, or public answer claims.

## Reconciliation packets

`feature/asoiaf-external-reconciliation-packets-v1` owns the bridge from reviewed external observations into the existing canon reconciliation formats. It freezes source identity, continuity, digest, locator, authority, rights, reviewer, candidate set, and decision surface in one packet. It cannot promote an observation that remains intake-only, lacks a claim-level receipt, crosses continuity without an explicit policy, or depends on community or analogue material as primary proof.

## Source health and credit control

`feature/asoiaf-external-source-health-v1` owns refresh planning, source availability, robots and terms drift, rights-review queues, no-result terminals, cache replay, source-ledger parity, and attribution debt. It schedules work without repeatedly hammering failed endpoints and emits receipts for every completed, deferred, blocked, unavailable, or retired source. Public credit remains a projection over the complete machine ledger rather than a substitute for it.

The branches may advance independently after the atlas passes its permanent qualification. Their only shared mutation surface is the atlas contract itself, which must return through this branch and its exact-count, deterministic-routing, authority, rights, and provenance tests.

The control question is whether acquisition, adjudication, and maintenance can each evolve without acquiring the authority owned by either of the other two planes.
